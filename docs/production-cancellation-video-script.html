Cancelling a Production — Screen Recording Guide

Shot-by-shot script for recording a ~3 minute training video. Narration given in English and Swahili.

Before you record

  • Use test data, not real production data. Create a practice batch with 2–3 products so nothing real gets cancelled on camera.
  • Log in as a user who has the cancel permission, otherwise the buttons won't appear.
  • Prepare a second batch that already has actual quantities recorded — you'll need it for Shot 9 (showing the refusal).
  • Note the ingredient stock figures before you start (Purchases → ingredient stock). You'll show them again after cancelling to prove the ingredients came back.
  • Close unrelated browser tabs, set browser zoom to 100%, and record at 1920×1080.
  • Hide any sensitive figures you don't want on a training video.
Tip: record the screen first, add narration after Doing the clicks silently and then recording the voice over the footage is far easier than trying to talk and click at the same time — and lets you re-record the narration in Swahili without re-doing the screen capture.

Shot list

Shot On screen / action Narration — English Narration — Swahili Time
1 Productions list open, showing a few batches. "When we plan a production, the system sets aside the ingredients it will need. But sometimes a production doesn't happen — the oven breaks, or staff don't come in. This video shows how to cancel it and get those ingredients back." "Tunapopanga uzalishaji, mfumo hutenga viungo vitakavyohitajika. Lakini wakati mwingine uzalishaji haufanyiki — oveni imeharibika, au wafanyakazi hawakufika. Video hii inaonyesha jinsi ya kughairi na kurudisha viungo hivyo." 0:00
2 Go to Purchases / ingredient stock. Point at the available quantity of one ingredient (e.g. Wheatflour). Pause on the number. "First, note the flour we have available right now — remember this number." "Kwanza, angalia unga tulionao sasa hivi — kumbuka namba hii." 0:20
3 Back to Productions. Click View Batch Details on the practice batch. "Now open the batch. Click View Batch Details." "Sasa fungua batch. Bonyeza View Batch Details." 0:35
4 Scroll to Products in Batch. Hover slowly over the row of the product that "wasn't made". "Here are the products in this batch. Let's say this one was never made — the dough didn't rise." "Hizi ndizo bidhaa zilizomo kwenye batch hii. Tuseme hii haikutengenezwa — donge halikuumuka." 0:45
5 Click the red Cancel button on that row. Dialog opens. "Click Cancel on that row. The system tells you exactly what will happen — this product will be cancelled, and its ingredients released back to stock." "Bonyeza Cancel kwenye safu hiyo. Mfumo unakuambia kitakachotokea — bidhaa hii itaghairiwa, na viungo vyake vitarudi stoo." 0:55
6 Type a reason slowly, e.g. "Dough did not rise". Then click Cancel & release ingredients. "Type a short reason. This isn't required, but it's saved with the record, so always write one — later you'll want to know why. Then confirm." "Andika sababu kwa ufupi. Si lazima, lakini inahifadhiwa kwenye kumbukumbu, hivyo andika kila wakati — baadaye utataka kujua kwa nini. Kisha thibitisha." 1:10
7 Dialog closes. Zoom/point at the grey Cancelled tag on the row. Hover it so the tooltip shows who/when/why. "The product now shows Cancelled. Notice it hasn't disappeared — it stays here with who cancelled it, when, and the reason. Cancelling is not deleting." "Bidhaa sasa inaonyesha Cancelled. Angalia haijapotea — inabaki hapa ikionyesha nani aliighairi, lini, na sababu. Kughairi si kufuta." 1:25
8 Go back to ingredient stock. Point at the same ingredient — the number has gone up. "And here's the important part — look at the flour again. It's gone back up. Those ingredients are free to use for another production today." "Na hapa ndipo muhimu — angalia unga tena. Umeongezeka. Viungo hivyo sasa vinaweza kutumika kwa uzalishaji mwingine leo." 1:40
9 Open the batch that already has actual quantities recorded. Show that the Cancel button is not there on a produced row. "One important rule. If actual production has already been recorded, you can't cancel it — see, there's no Cancel button here. The bread really exists, and may already be at a shop. To fix a mistake there, edit the actual quantities instead." "Kanuni moja muhimu. Kama kiasi halisi tayari kimeingizwa, huwezi kughairi — angalia, hakuna kitufe cha Cancel hapa. Mikate ipo kweli, na huenda tayari iko dukani. Kurekebisha kosa hapo, hariri kiasi halisi badala yake." 1:55
10 Back to the practice batch. Click Cancel Batch at the top right. Show the dialog, then confirm. "If the whole day was called off, use Cancel Batch at the top instead. That cancels everything still remaining in the batch and releases all their ingredients at once." "Kama uzalishaji wote wa siku ulisitishwa, tumia Cancel Batch juu badala yake. Hiyo inaghairi kila kilichobaki kwenye batch na kurudisha viungo vyote kwa mara moja." 2:15
11 Show the batch now marked Cancelled in the batch list. "The whole batch is now marked Cancelled." "Batch nzima sasa imewekwa alama ya Cancelled." 2:35
12 Return to Productions list. Hold still for the closing line. "So: cancel when a planned production didn't happen, and the ingredients come straight back. If it was made — even partly — record the actual quantity instead. Always write a reason." "Kwa hiyo: ghairi pale mpango wa uzalishaji haukutekelezwa, na viungo vitarudi papo hapo. Kama ulitengenezwa — hata kwa sehemu — ingiza kiasi halisi badala yake. Andika sababu kila wakati." 2:45

Recording tips

  • Move the mouse slowly. Fast cursor movement is the most common reason training videos are hard to follow.
  • Pause 2 seconds after every click before moving on, so viewers can see what changed.
  • Say button names exactly as they appear ("View Batch Details", not "the details button") — the app is in English even when narrating in Swahili.
  • Shots 2 and 8 are the most valuable in the whole video. Showing the stock number before and after is what makes people trust the feature. Don't rush them.
  • If you fluff a line, pause, then repeat the whole sentence — it's much easier to cut in editing than a half-sentence.
